\begin{abstract}
High-resolution analysis of maps on the sphere is limited by the computational cost of spherical harmonic transforms (SHTs). We introduce a difference-form Wigner-$d$/Jacobi recurrence that suppresses the amplification of floating-point error and makes accurate single-precision transforms practical on a GPU. The functions are generated on the fly, so the full latitudinal basis is never stored. The method is implemented in \texttt{GMaster}\,\href{https://github.com/licongxu/GMaster}{\faGithub}, with CUDA kernels integrated with JAX. On one GPU, float32 \texttt{map2alm} at $N_{\mathrm{side}}=1024$--$2048$ is $7.3$--$8.5\times$ faster for spin~0 and $6.7$--$9.0\times$ faster for spin~2 than \texttt{ducc0} on 96 CPU cores, and $2.2$--$4.7\times$ faster than float64 \texttt{SHTns} on the HEALPix grid for spin-0 \texttt{map2alm} over $N_{\mathrm{side}}=512$--$8192$. On the ACT DR6 maps the float32 difference-form march carries the masked maps to decoupled bandpowers $12$--$22\times$ faster than 96-core \texttt{NaMaster} for $N_{\mathrm{side}}=512$--$8192$, with the bandpowers agreeing to $5.7\times10^{-5}$ in TT and $3.6\times10^{-4}$ in EE at $N_{\mathrm{side}}=4096$. The same march evaluates the curved-sky CMB lensing quadratic estimator. At $\ell_{\max}=2000$ and $4000$ the seven estimators take $0.103\,\mathrm{s}$ and $0.594\,\mathrm{s}$, $61\times$ and $41\times$ faster than \texttt{falafel} on its CAR grid and $96.5\times$ and $64\times$ faster than \texttt{falafel} on HEALPix, agreeing with the HEALPix reconstruction to $2.8\times10^{-5}$ and $7.0\times10^{-5}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Spherical harmonic transforms (SHTs) are a fundamental numerical operation in the analysis of data on spheres. They are widely used in cosmology, including the analyses of cosmic microwave background (CMB) and large-scale structure. Scalar observables such as CMB temperature and projected density fields require spin-0 transforms, while CMB polarization and weak lensing shear are described by spin-2 fields. SHTs therefore enter repeatedly in map filtering, inverse-variance operations, lensing reconstructions, and pseudo-$C_\ell$ power-spectrum estimation. As modern cosmological surveys move to higher resolution and increasingly rely on large suites of simulations, the computational cost of these transforms has become an important component of these analyses.

% Angular power spectra are one of the standard and most useful statistics for extracting cosmological information from observations of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) and large-scale structure. In practice, cosmological observations cover only part of the sky and require masks to exclude foreground-contaminated regions, point sources, survey boundaries, and other unusable data. Masking induces mode coupling in spherical harmonic space, leading to a bias on the naive estimate of the underlying power spectrum. 

% The pseudo-$C_\ell$ approach \citep{hivon2002} provides an efficient framework for correcting this mode coupling by relating spectra measured on the masked sky to the underlying full-sky spectra through a mask-dependent mode-coupling matrix. Its computational cost scales as $\mathcal{O}(\ell_{\max}^3)$, where $\ell_{\max}$ denotes the maximum multipole included in the analysis, compared with the substantially steeper $\mathcal{O}(\ell_{\max}^6)$ scaling of exact pixel-space likelihood methods. \texttt{NaMaster} \citep{alonso2019} implements the pseudo-$C_\ell$ formalism as a general software package for scalar and spin fields, including angular auto- and cross-power spectra.

On iso-latitude pixelizations such as HEALPix \citep{gorski2005}, the azimuthal part of an SHT can be evaluated using Fourier transforms, while the latitudinal part requires repeated evaluation of associated Legendre functions, or more generally Wigner-$d$ functions for spin fields. The dominant cost lies in the evaluation of Wigner-$d$ functions over a large number of harmonic modes and sky rings. Modern SHT libraries generate them on the fly using recurrence relations, in which the functions at a given harmonic degree are obtained from those at neighboring degrees. This approach is highly optimized in high-performance CPU implementations. \texttt{libsharp}, and subsequently \texttt{DUCC} \citep{reinecke2013}, combine on the fly recurrence with single-instruction, multiple-data (SIMD) vectorization and multithreading, while \texttt{DUCC} further incorporates the accelerated recurrence of \citet{ishioka2018}. \texttt{NaMaster}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/LSSTDESC/NaMaster}} \citep{alonso2019} uses \texttt{DUCC} for scalar and spin SHTs within its pseudo-$C_\ell$ estimator. \texttt{SHTns} \citep{schaeffer2013,ishioka2018} follows a similar recurrence-based approach, combining on the fly evaluation with vectorization, multithreading, optimized FFTs, and GPU backends for NVIDIA and AMD hardware.

More recently, several approaches have been developed specifically to accelerate SHTs on GPUs and other accelerator hardware. \texttt{S2FFT} \citep{price2024} implements differentiable scalar and spin transforms in JAX using a highly parallel Wigner-$d$ recurrence. The Wigner-$d$ functions can either be precomputed and stored for faster evaluation, or generated on the fly using the recurrence to reduce memory requirements. \texttt{cunuSHT} \citep{belkner2024} targets transforms on arbitrary and non-uniform grids, combining a regular-grid SHT with a double-Fourier-sphere construction and non-uniform FFTs, with applications to CMB lensing and remapping. \texttt{cuHPX} \citep{cheng2025cuhpxgpuaccelerateddifferentiablespherical} provides differentiable GPU transforms tailored to HEALPix, using precomputed associated-Legendre functions together with GPU ring FFTs and tensor contractions, with an out-of-core strategy for handling the resulting basis tables at high resolution. A different approach is taken by \texttt{parallALT} \citep{tolmachev2026}, which converts associated-Legendre expansions to Chebyshev expansions and evaluates them using GPU-accelerated discrete cosine and sine transforms, reporting speed-ups of up to a factor of three over \texttt{SHTns} on an NVIDIA H100.

These implementations expose a trade-off between memory use and numerical precision. Precomputing the Wigner-$d$ functions avoids evaluating the recurrence during each transform, but the required memory grows rapidly with resolution. The recurrence-based method therefore remains competitive at high resolution, with \texttt{SHTns}\footnote{\url{https://bitbucket.org/nschaeff/shtns}} providing a state-of-the-art publicly available implementation of this approach on both CPUs and GPUs. \texttt{SHTns} is primarily designed around double-precision arithmetic to maintain the accuracy of the recurrence relations. On GPUs, however, single-precision arithmetic can offer substantially higher throughput. Moreover, \texttt{SHTns} does not provide the spin-2 transforms required for CMB polarization. General-spin GPU transforms are available in \texttt{S2FFT}, but high-resolution calculations with comparable accuracy similarly rely on 64-bit arithmetic. Efficient use of modern GPU hardware therefore requires SHT algorithms that can exploit lower-precision arithmetic to increase computational throughput while maintaining the numerical accuracy of high-resolution transforms.

In this Letter, we introduce a difference-form reformulation of the standard Wigner-$d$ recurrence that enables accurate single-precision evaluation at high band limits. Our approach retains the established structure of recurrence-based SHTs, with Fourier transforms along iso-latitude rings and on-the-fly evaluation of the Wigner-$d$ functions through a recurrence in harmonic degree, as used in \texttt{libsharp}/\texttt{DUCC} and \texttt{SHTns} \citep{reinecke2013,schaeffer2013,ishioka2018}. The new formulation replaces the standard recurrence by its difference form, suppressing the loss of numerical accuracy encountered in direct single-precision evaluation while retaining the same recurrence-based SHT algorithm. We then implement the method in \texttt{GMaster}, a GPU-native framework for scalar and spin spherical harmonic transforms using custom CUDA kernels integrated with JAX. Section~\ref{sec:formalism} presents the recurrence formulation and implementation, Section~\ref{sec:performance} gives the numerical validation and performance benchmarks, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarizes our conclusions.

Exact spin-weighted transforms, in both the analysis and synthesis directions, can also be built from Wigner-$d$ functions at a single colatitude together with fast convolution on the sphere \citep{wandelt2001,huffenberger2010}. On GPUs, \texttt{cunuSHT} treats arbitrary pixelizations by a double-Fourier-sphere method and non-uniform FFTs, taking the iso-latitude ring transform from \texttt{SHTns} \citep{belkner2024}, while \texttt{parallALT} uses a fully spectral algorithm and reports speed-ups of up to a factor of three over \texttt{SHTns} on an NVIDIA H100 \citep{tolmachev2026}. We keep the on-the-fly recurrence, which stores no latitudinal basis, and rewrite its carried state in difference form. The standard recurrence loses the accuracy required for a high-resolution transform when it is evaluated directly in single precision; the difference form recovers that accuracy for both the forward and inverse transforms, while remaining in the single-precision arithmetic that makes the recurrence inexpensive on a GPU.

\section{Formalism and Implementation}
\label{sec:formalism}
In this section, we review the mathematical formulation of the spherical harmonic transform. We then derive a numerically stable recurrence relations for its efficient and accurate evaluation in single precision, and describe its implementation on the GPU.

A field of integer spin $s$ on the sphere is expanded in spin-weighted spherical harmonics as
${}_s f(\theta,\phi)=\sum_{\ell m}{}_s f_{\ell m}\,{}_sY_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)$ for $\ell \ge |s|$ and $|m| \le \ell$, where $\theta\in[0,\pi]$ is the colatitude measured from the north pole, the latitude is $\pi/2-\theta$, $\phi\in[0,2\pi)$ is the azimuth and the longitude, $\ell$ is the multipole degree, $m$ is the azimuthal order and $\ell_{\max}$ is the highest multipole retained in a transform. These basis functions relate directly to the Wigner little-$d$ matrix element via ${}_s Y_{\ell m}(\theta, \phi) = (-1)^m \sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{4\pi}} d^\ell_{m, -s}(\theta) e^{im\phi}$. On an iso-latitude grid, the azimuthal dependence is handled by Fourier transforms along each ring, leaving the repeated evaluation of the Wigner-$d$ functions in the colatitude direction. Every Wigner function $d^\ell_{m,-s}(\theta)$ is an algebraic multiple of a Jacobi polynomial \citep[NIST DLMF 14.9.16, 18.3]{olver2010},
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  d^\ell_{m,-s}(\theta)
  &= (-1)^{m+s}\,2^{\varepsilon_m\mathrm{LG2N}}\\
  &\quad\times
    \Bigl(\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\Bigr)^\alpha
    \Bigl(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\Bigr)^\beta
    P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\cos\theta),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha=m+s$, $\beta=\lvert m-s\rvert$, $n=\ell-\max(m,s)$,
$\varepsilon_m=+1$ for $m\ge s$ and $-1$ otherwise, and
$\mathrm{LG2N}=\log_2\sqrt{(\ell+m)!(\ell-m)!/((\ell-s)!(\ell+s)!)}$. The family obeys a three-term recurrence in $n$ \citep[DLMF 18.9.1]{olver2010}. Writing $x=\cos\theta$ and $v_n=P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)$, the Jacobi sequence obeys the standard three-term recurrence \citep[DLMF 18.9.2]{olver2010},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jacobi}
v_n=A_n v_{n-1}-B_n v_{n-2},
\qquad
A_n=c_{1,n}x+c_{0,n},
\quad
B_n=c_{b,n},
\end{equation}
where $c_{1,n}$, $c_{0,n}$ and $c_{b,n}$ are the standard Jacobi recurrence coefficients.
% A float64 march of~\eqref{eq:jacobi} agrees with a numpy oracle of the same coefficients to $1.5\times10^{-15}$ at $N_{\mathrm{side}}=1024$. Plain float32 of the same recurrence reaches a maximum relative error of $4.2\times10^{-2}$ over the row (Table~\ref{tab:dform}).

For fixed $(m,s)$, the Jacobi recurrence coefficients vary slowly in the high-degree limit: asymptotically,
$c_{1,n}=2-n^{-1}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-2})$,
$c_{b,n}=1-n^{-1}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-2})$, and
$c_{0,n}=\mathcal{O}(n^{-2})$.
Hence their change over one recurrence step is $\mathcal{O}(n^{-2})$, allowing a local constant-coefficient analysis over intervals $\Delta n\ll n$. Writing
\begin{equation}
v_n\simeq A v_{n-1}-Bv_{n-2},
\end{equation}
the characteristic roots in the oscillatory regime are
$r=\sqrt{B}\,e^{\pm i\phi}$, with
$A=2\sqrt{B}\cos\phi$. This angle $\phi$ is the phase of those characteristic roots. Consider a rounding error $\delta_k$ introduced in $v_k$, with $v_{k-1}$ unchanged. Under the local constant-coefficient approximation, its contribution after $n-k$ subsequent steps is
\begin{equation}
\Delta v_n
\simeq
\delta_k\,B^{(n-k)/2}
\frac{\sin[(n-k+1)\phi]}{\sin\phi}.
\end{equation}
Since $B\simeq1$ at high degree, each local perturbation can be amplified
by a factor of order $1/|\sin\phi|$. For approximately independent
floating-point errors of characteristic size $u|v|$ accumulated over $N$
recurrence steps, the relative error scales as
$\lvert\Delta v\rvert/|v|\sim\sqrt{N}\,u/|\sin\phi|$, where $u$ is the
unit roundoff ($u=2^{-24}$ for IEEE float32). The amplification becomes
large when the two characteristic roots approach degeneracy,
$A^2\simeq4B$, for which $|\sin\phi|\ll1$. For the scalar case, this
corresponds asymptotically to the turning condition
$|m|\simeq(\ell+\tfrac12)\sin\theta$. Since an SHT spans the full range
$|m|\leq\ell$, its required mode space includes modes close to this
condition, making the standard single-precision recurrence poorly
conditioned there. 
% This motivates the difference-form reformulation below.

We therefore introduce the difference
$D_n=v_n-v_{n-1}$. Substituting
$v_{n-2}=v_{n-1}-D_{n-1}$ into Eq.~\eqref{eq:jacobi} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:diff}
D_n=C_n v_{n-1}+B_nD_{n-1},
\qquad
v_n=v_{n-1}+D_n,
\end{equation}
where $C_n=A_n-1-B_n$. Near the $\phi\rightarrow0$ degeneracy,
$B\simeq1$, $C=\mathcal{O}(\phi^2)$, and
$D_n=\mathcal{O}(\phi v)$. Provided that $C_n$ is evaluated accurately,
the floating-point error introduced in the $D_n$ update is therefore
$\mathcal{O}(u\phi|v|)$. An error in $D_k$ enters $v_k$ with
$\Delta v_k\neq0$ and $\Delta v_{k-1}=0$, and hence follows the
$1/|\sin\phi|$ propagation derived above. Since
$|\sin\phi|\simeq|\phi|$ in this limit, the propagated error remains
$\mathcal{O}(u|v|)$ instead of being enhanced by
$1/|\sin\phi|$.

A rounding of $v\leftarrow\mathrm{fl}(v+D)$ lands on $v$ with $D$ fixed and is not amplified. A rounding of $D\leftarrow\mathrm{fl}(c_2 D+C v)$ lands on $D$ at scale $u\phi\lvert v\rvert$ and, after the $1/(2\sin\phi)$ modal factor, contributes $O(u\lvert v\rvert)$ per step. Both kinds therefore give a random-walk floor $\sqrt{n}\,u$ independent of $\phi$: $\sqrt{3072}\,u\approx3.3\times10^{-6}$ at $N_{\mathrm{side}}=1024$, against the measured $2.8\times10^{-6}$.

The error reduction above relies on resolving the small coefficient
$C_n$ accurately. Near the $\phi\rightarrow0$ degeneracy,
$C_n=\mathcal{O}(\phi^2)$, but its direct form,
\[
C_n=A_n-1-B_n
    =c_{1,n}x+c_{0,n}-1-c_{b,n},
\]
subtracts quantities of order unity. In float32, the rounding error in
these terms can therefore exceed the value of $C_n$ itself and reintroduce
an $\mathcal{O}(u|v|)$ error into the difference update. We avoid this
cancellation by writing
\begin{equation}
C_n=c_{1,n}(x-1)+E_n^+,
\qquad
E_n^+=c_{1,n}+c_{0,n}-1-c_{b,n}.
\end{equation}
Near the northern pole, $x-1$ is small, while the high-degree expansions above give $E_n^+=\mathcal{O}(n^{-2})$. Thus $C_n$ is constructed from
small quantities rather than obtained as the residual of order-unity
terms.

To preserve these quantities in the float32 kernel, they are first
evaluated in float64 and represented as high--low float32 pairs,
$z\simeq z_{\rm h}+z_{\rm l}$, where $z_{\rm h}$ is the leading float32
part and $z_{\rm l}$ stores the remaining residual
\citep{dekker1971}. The GPU kernel combines the two parts using fused
multiply--add (FMA) operations, which evaluate $ab+c$ with a single final
rounding. This prevents the evaluation of $C_n$ from introducing an
$\mathcal{O}(u|v|)$ error while the recurrence variables $(v_n,D_n)$
remain in float32. The coefficient $B_n=c_{b,n}$ is kept in a single
float32 value because its rounding error multiplies
$D_{n-1}=\mathcal{O}(\phi v)$ and therefore introduces only an
$\mathcal{O}(u\phi|v|)$ perturbation.

The analysis above applies to the $\phi\rightarrow0$ branch, for which
the characteristic roots approach $+1$. For the opposite branch,
$\phi\rightarrow\pi$, the roots approach $-1$ and consecutive values
alternate in sign. Defining $w_n=(-1)^n v_n$ maps this branch onto the
same difference recurrence, with $x$ replaced by $|x|$ and
$c_{0,n}$ changing sign. Northern and southern rings can therefore be
marched by the same kernel using
$E_n^\pm=c_{1,n}-1-c_{b,n}\pm c_{0,n}$, with the factor $(-1)^n$
restored on output.

The latitudinal transform is implemented as a custom CUDA kernel designed around the sequential structure of the recurrence. Each warp is assigned one azimuthal order $m$ and a tile of iso-latitude rings. The rings are processed in parallel, while each thread advances the recurrence sequentially in $\ell$ and retains only the current recurrence variables. At each step, the newly generated Wigner-$d$ value is immediately multiplied by the corresponding ring Fourier coefficient and accumulated into the harmonic coefficient, so the full Wigner-$d$ basis is never stored. The custom kernels are called directly from JAX-compiled code, keeping the full transform and its intermediate arrays on the GPU.

For the scalar coupling, we use the exact Wigner-$3j$ formulation of \citet{kiddier2026}, to which we refer the reader for a detailed derivation of the recurrence relations and their application to pseudo-$C_\ell$ coupling matrices. \texttt{GMaster} reorganizes the resulting sum for GPU execution by parameterizing the allowed configurations with an offset $o$. At fixed $o$, only the trailing $(N_\ell-o)\times(N_\ell-o)$ block contributes, reducing the number of visited matrix elements to $\sum_o (N_\ell-o)^2\simeq N_\ell^3/3$, where $N_\ell=\ell_{\max}+1$. For mixed and spin couplings, we instead rewrite the Wigner-$3j$ sums as one-dimensional integrals over products of Wigner-$d$ functions. The mask multipole sum is first transformed into an angular correlation function evaluated on Gauss--Legendre nodes, after which the coupling matrix becomes a weighted contraction of two recurrence-generated Wigner-$d$ tables. The remaining $(\ell_1,\ell_2)$ calculation is therefore reduced to dense matrix multiplications on the GPU, while parity-even and parity-odd couplings are obtained simultaneously from the corresponding parity-weighted mask correlations.

\section{Benchmark and Performance}
\label{sec:performance}

Here, we assess the numerical accuracy and computational performance of the SHTs using different algorithms and hardware platforms. A \texttt{map2alm} transform converts a pixel-space scalar or spin field into spherical-harmonic coefficients, while \texttt{alm2map} performs the inverse synthesis.

We benchmark \texttt{GMaster} using the ACT DR6 night PA4 $f150$ source-free Stokes maps shown in Figure~\ref{fig:act_tqu} \citep{naess2025}. To benchmark over a range of resolutions, the native $N_{\mathrm{side}}=8192$ maps are downgraded to each $N_{\mathrm{side}}$ from $64$ to $4096$ by averaging their child pixels. At every resolution, the maps are multiplied by the binary ACT footprint ($f_{\mathrm{sky}}=0.481$ at native resolution). In the spin-0 case, the masked temperature map $T$ is transformed into its scalar spherical-harmonic coefficients $a^T_{\ell m}$. In the spin-2 case, the masked polarization maps $(Q,U)$ are transformed into the corresponding $E$- and $B$-mode coefficients $(a^E_{\ell m},a^B_{\ell m})$. These masked-sky coefficients are then used to form the pseudo-spectra, with the mode-coupling matrix correcting the mixing introduced by the mask, including $E$--$B$ mixing in the spin-2 case.

We compare \texttt{GMaster} against \texttt{SHTns} on the same single NVIDIA RTX~PRO~6000 Blackwell GPU, and against \texttt{NaMaster} with the \texttt{ducc0} backend on an AMD Threadripper~PRO~9995WX using 32 and 96 CPU cores. All three transform the HEALPix grid. \texttt{SHTns} handles only grids with the same number of longitudes on every ring, so we run its GPU Legendre kernels on the HEALPix ring latitudes and weights and perform the HEALPix ring FFTs ourselves, with a chirp-$z$ transform for the polar rings. \texttt{SHTns} has no spin-2 transform. We build one from its scalar and vector kernels with the identity $\eth^2 Y_{\ell m}=-2(\cot\theta+m/\sin\theta)\,\partial_\theta Y_{\ell m}+\left[2m\cot\theta/\sin\theta+2m^2/\sin^2\theta-\ell(\ell+1)\right]Y_{\ell m}$ and its conjugate, whose $1/\sin\theta$ factors cancel analytically at the poles but not in floating point. \texttt{GMaster} uses the float32 difference-form recurrence described in Section~\ref{sec:formalism}. For \texttt{SHTns}, we time float64 throughout and float32 throughout; in the latter the recurrence itself is float32 (its \texttt{SHT\_FP32} mode otherwise keeps the recurrence in float64 on this GPU). The \texttt{SHTns} timings were taken on a second, identical RTX~PRO~6000 in the same machine as the \texttt{GMaster} timings.

\section{Applications}
\label{sec:application}
We now demonstrate the use of SHTs with GMaster in cosmological analysis workflows. We consider two applications: pseudo-$C_\ell$ power-spectrum estimation from masked CMB maps and CMB lensing reconstruction with the quadratic estimator. We compare the numerical accuracy and computational cost of \texttt{GMaster} with \texttt{NaMaster} for the pseudo-$C_\ell$ calculation and with \texttt{falafel} for the lensing reconstruction.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We have introduced a difference-form Jacobi recurrence for accurate single-precision evaluation of Wigner-$d$ functions in high-resolution spherical harmonic transforms. By suppressing the $1/|\sin\phi|$ amplification of floating-point errors and accurately evaluating the small recurrence coefficient, the method retains the low-memory, on-the-fly structure of recurrence-based SHTs while remaining efficient in float32 on GPUs.

The float32 difference-form march is the transform timed in Section~\ref{sec:performance}, and it is the latitudinal engine used for the measurements in this letter. On one RTX~PRO~6000, \texttt{map2alm} at $N_{\mathrm{side}}=1024$--$2048$ is $7.3$--$8.5\times$ faster for spin~0 and $6.7$--$9.0\times$ faster for spin~2 than \texttt{ducc0} on 96 CPU cores, and $2.2$--$4.7\times$ faster than float64 HEALPix \texttt{SHTns} for spin-0 \texttt{map2alm} over $N_{\mathrm{side}}=512$--$8192$. On the ACT DR6 maps the path from masked maps to decoupled bandpowers is $12$--$22\times$ faster than 96-core \texttt{NaMaster} for $N_{\mathrm{side}}=512$--$8192$, with TT and EE agreeing to $5.7\times10^{-5}$ and $3.6\times10^{-4}$ at $N_{\mathrm{side}}=4096$. The same march evaluates the lensing quadratic estimator. At $\ell_{\max}=2000$ and $4000$ the seven estimators take $0.103\,\mathrm{s}$ and $0.594\,\mathrm{s}$, $61\times$ and $41\times$ faster than \texttt{falafel} on CAR and $96.5\times$ and $64\times$ faster than \texttt{falafel} on HEALPix, agreeing with the HEALPix run to $2.8\times10^{-5}$ and $7.0\times10^{-5}$. The implementation is publicly available as \texttt{GMaster}.
